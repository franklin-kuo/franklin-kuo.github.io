PROJECT 01

Survey → Civil 3D
Automation Suite

Engineering Intern · Civil engineering consultancy · Summer 2026

  • StatusDelivered & audited
PASS 100.00% of points within 0.05 ft

The problem

Turning a raw survey point file into track geometry that Civil 3D will accept is slow, manual, and unforgiving. An engineer separates the tracks by hand, fits tangents, curves and spirals by eye, and has no systematic way to prove the result is within tolerance before it reaches design.

What I built

A wizard-driven tool that takes a standard PNEZD export, separates the tracks, fits real horizontal geometry, and writes a LandXML file that imports straight into Civil 3D — alongside an Excel review report an engineer signs off: verdict tab, curve and PI table, point-by-point ledger, and drawings.

The part I am proudest of

It grades its own output and refuses to lie about it. Every run ends PASS, DRAFT, or NOT APPROVED — and a failing file is renamed on disk so it physically cannot be imported by accident.

The acceptance bar is stated verbatim in every report: ≥98% of points within 0.20 ft, every miss flagged, zero kinks, XML round-trip validated. A run that misses the bar says so on the front page. That is the tool working, not the tool breaking.

Also in the suite

Incident Sorter

Sorts raw grade-crossing incident reports into a 44-sheet matrix workbook, 28 categories per sheet. An independent checker re-derives the result: 11,389 rows reconcile.

Gate Queue Sorter

Files every train-observation row into the right crossing tab across 43 crossing sheets, with a run log of exactly what went where.

Master Graph Creator

Drives Excel itself to add three hourly charts to 43 workbooks without destroying the charts already in them. Shipped 1.0.1 after I found a bug where it could close a user's other open workbooks — now covered by six regression tests.

Two further utilities — a volume converter and a file-usage tracker — round out the six-tool handover.

What it is

The occupant cell is the structure the driver sits inside — the volume that has to stay intact when nothing else does. I designed this on the occupant cell subteam within our chassis team.

The straight-wall revision

This is revision 21, the straight-wall attempt. The side walls were flattened from the previous curved profile — a change that trades a small amount of formed stiffness for manufacturability and a cleaner interface with the surrounding chassis members.

Envelope

The cell measures 914.40 mm across — exactly 36.00 inches, a driven dimension rather than an accident of the loft — by 505.12 mm deep and 1666.60 mm tall.
